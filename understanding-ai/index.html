DEPTH0 m

An AI model is not magic, and it is not a mind. It is a very large file of numbers that learned patterns from huge amounts of data, plus software that runs it. Once you understand those two pieces, everything else, from who makes these models to how you can run one privately at home, starts to make sense.

This guide walks through the whole picture in order: what models are and who makes them, how to compare and run them yourself, how to keep that setup private, how AI agents are shaped, and where the field and its careers are heading. Each part builds on the one before. Every section ends with a set of key terms, and a quick self-check waits at the bottom.

Snapshot warning

AI changes month to month. Model names, rankings, and licenses here reflect late 2026. Check the maker's official page before relying on a detail.

A · Foundations
01

How AI models work

An AI model is a program that learned patterns from huge amounts of data. A large language model (LLM) learned mainly from text (often also code and images). It writes by predicting the next small chunk of text, a token, over and over.

What it learned is stored as billions of numbers called weights (also called parameters). "Downloading a model" means downloading those weight files. Model size is given in parameters: "8B" means 8 billion.

StageWhat happensCost / who
TrainingThe model learns from a massive dataset; its weights are adjusted over and over.Very expensive. Done by big labs on thousands of GPUs (the fast chips that train and run models) in data centers.
Fine-tuningExtra training of an existing model on a smaller, focused dataset to change its skills or style. LoRA is a cheap method that trains a small add-on instead of every weight.Affordable. Companies and hobbyists do it; small models can be tuned on one good GPU.
InferenceUsing the finished model to answer. Weights do not change.Everyday use, in chat apps or on your own PC.

Chatting with a model is inference, not training. The model file itself does not change.

If you use a cloud app, the company may still keep your chats, or use them to train later models, unless you turn that off. When an app seems to "remember" you, it saved notes and fed them back in as extra text. Section 9 explains how to use this on purpose.

Key terms
Model
A trained AI program.
LLM
A large language model: a model for language.
Token
A chunk of text, often part of a word.
Weights / parameters
The learned numbers.
Training / fine-tuning / inference
Learn from scratch / adjust / use.
02

Open vs. closed models

TypeWhat you getExamples
ClosedUse only through the maker's app or paid API (a way for programs to talk to a model); weights are secret.Flagship models from the largest commercial labs
Open-weightDownload and run the weights yourself. Training data and full recipe usually not shared. License rules vary.Llama, Gemma, Qwen, DeepSeek, Mistral, gpt-oss
Fully openWeights plus training data, code, and details, so anyone can study or reproduce it.OLMo (AI2)

Licenses: the rules for using the weights

  • Apache 2.0: permissive. Use, modify, and sell freely; keep the notice. It also includes a patent grant.
  • MIT: very short and very permissive. Keep the copyright notice.
  • Llama license: Meta's custom community license. It is free for most uses but adds an acceptable-use policy, attribution rules, and a separate-license requirement for very large companies.
  • Other custom or non-commercial licenses add limits. Read the license on the model page before any business use.

04

Customizing and trusting models

Do you have to rely on big-company models?

Mostly yes, for the base model. Training a strong model from scratch takes resources only large labs have. But you can customize a lot on top:

ApproachWhat it doesEffort
Community modelsThousands of fine-tuned variants (coding, writing, other languages) shared on Hugging Face.Download and try
RAGThe system looks up your own documents and adds relevant passages to the model's input, so answers use your data. The model itself is unchanged.Moderate; the most common business approach
Fine-tuning / LoRATrains the model further on your examples to change style or skills.Higher; needs data and a GPU
Context engineeringInstructions, examples, and memory supplied with each request (Section 9).Low; start here

Hugging Face

Hugging Face is the main hub for sharing models, datasets, and demos, often called the "GitHub of AI." It is named after the 🤗 hugging-face emoji and began in 2016 as a chatbot app for teenagers before switching to open-source AI tools.

Can a model file hide malicious code?

FormatWhat it isRisk
safetensorsModern format that stores only numbersSafe: it cannot run code when loaded. Prefer it.
pickle (.bin/.pt)Older Python save formatLoading one can run hidden code. Use only from trusted sources.
GGUFSingle-file format from llama.cpp, usually quantizedA data-only format; still download from reputable uploaders.

The weights themselves are just numbers. The bigger risks are unsafe file formats and the software around the model. That's one reason open-source runners help: anyone can inspect their code.

05

Comparing models: what makes one "better"

FactorWhy it matters
Parameters (size)Bigger is usually smarter but needs more memory and runs slower.
QuantizationCompressed versions (e.g., 4-bit) are smaller and faster, with slight quality loss.
SpecialtyCoding, reasoning, vision, or language-specific models beat general ones at their specialty.
AgeNewer small models often beat older, bigger ones. Last year's leader may be average today.
Context windowHow much text it can consider at once. Some frontier models advertise a million tokens or more, but quality can drop near the limit, and long context needs a lot of memory locally.
Speed / costTokens per second; API price per token or the hardware needed.

How the "AI race" is scored

  • Benchmarks are standardized tests. MMLU covers broad knowledge across ~57 subjects. GPQA is hard graduate-level science. SWE-bench asks models to fix real GitHub bugs. AIME uses contest math.
  • LMArena (formerly Chatbot Arena): people vote between two anonymous answers. Votes become Elo-style ratings, like chess, where beating strong opponents earns more points. It measures preference, which includes style.
Caution

Benchmarks can be gamed. Test questions can leak into training data, labs tune for popular tests, and companies highlight their best scores. The most reliable test is your own: try candidates on your real tasks.

06

Running models locally

The single biggest constraint is VRAM, the memory on the graphics card (GPU). The model has to fit in VRAM to run fast. If it doesn't, part of it spills into regular RAM and runs much slower.

Model sizeRough memory at 4-bit (Q4)Typical hardware
~7–8B~5–6 GB8–12 GB GPUs; many Apple Silicon laptops
~14–32B~9–20 GB16–24 GB GPUs; a sweet spot for home use
70B+~40 GB and upMultiple GPUs, workstation cards, or a high-memory Mac
Rule of thumb

About 0.5–0.6 GB per billion parameters at Q4, plus extra for context. Full 16-bit precision needs about 2 GB per billion.

C · Privacy & security
08

Privacy & security

How private is "local"?

With local inference, prompts and files are processed on your own machine, not a company's servers. But apps may still send telemetry (usage data), and anything with web search, downloads, or plugins reaches the internet. Local is private only if you control those connections.

Can network traffic be hidden?

Not made invisible, but it can be made unreadable and limited:

TechniqueWhat it does
Encryption (HTTPS, VPN, Tailscale)HTTPS hides the contents, but your internet provider or a Wi-Fi snoop can still see that you send data and which site you are talking to. A VPN (or Tailscale) also hides that destination; the ISP only sees traffic to the VPN server.
Firewall / outbound blockingStops the box talking to the internet except for what you allow.
DNS filtering (Pi-hole)Blocks known tracking and telemetry addresses for the whole network, and shows what each device tries to contact.
VLAN / guest networkPuts the AI box in its own network zone so other devices can't see or reach it. Many routers' guest networks do a basic version.
Tailscale, not port forwardingPort forwarding opens your box to the whole internet. Tailscale creates a private encrypted network among your devices only.
Air gapNo network at all; files move by USB. Maximum isolation, least convenient.

Split roles: a standard security method

Never keep the most valuable data on the machine that faces the internet.

Security professionals call this segmentation, layered as defense in depth, with least privilege (each part gets only the access it needs). Banks, hospitals, and militaries work this way.

Box A · PrivateModel, documents, memory. No internet, or updates only.
⇄
Narrow bridgeOnly specific requests pass (e.g., a web page as plain text)
⇄
Box B · Public-facingWeb search, email, downloads. Nothing valuable stored.

If Box B is compromised, the attacker lands somewhere with nothing worth stealing. Box A never browses the web itself. With Proxmox, both "boxes" can be virtual machines on one physical computer.

Isolation toolWhat it isStrength
Container (Docker)A walled-off package for one app, sharing the host's operating systemLight, fast, easy to rebuild
Virtual machine (VM)A complete simulated computer with its own OSStronger isolation; heavier

AI agents & context engineering

A chatbot answers one message. An agent works toward a goal: it plans steps, uses tools (web search, email, files, code), checks results, and continues until done or until it needs a human. Assistant bots with tools, memory, and scheduled routines are agents.

Using an agent does not train it. You shape its behavior through what it sees each time it works.

That is context engineering, a fuller way to think about "prompting." It has five main levers:

LeverWhat it isUse for
InstructionsStanding role and rules read every timeMission, tone, boundaries, default format
MemorySaved facts recalled laterPreferences, decisions, key details
SkillsSaved step-by-step recipesRepeatable multi-step tasks
Tools / connectorsAbilities to fetch real data or actCurrent facts instead of guesses
ExamplesSamples of good outputMatching format and detail

Best practices for shaping agents

  1. One clear job per agent: e.g., one for household tasks, one for research, one for finances. Also state what it does not do.
  2. Correct right away, specifically: "Next time, keep summaries to 5 bullets with a source link." Then ask it to save the lesson to memory.
  3. Turn a well-done task into a skill with a trigger (when to use), inputs, numbered steps, an output format, and stop rules.
  4. Show examples of the output you want, and define the format.
  5. Set boundaries: it may search, read, and draft on its own, but must ask before sending messages, spending money, deleting, or sharing.
  6. Routines: run on a schedule or trigger, and stay quiet when nothing changed.
  7. Prune memory regularly. Stale or wrong facts steer it wrong.
  8. Test: keep a few test cases, rerun the same task to check consistency, and retest after changes.
FailureFix
Hallucination (confident false facts)Require sources opened during the task; give it real-data tools.
OverconfidenceInstruct it to state uncertainty and how it would check.
Scope creepTighten the mission; add stop rules.
Stale memoryRegular memory review; update when things change.
Prompt injection (hostile text in pages or emails)Boundaries that require approval for risky actions.

Data centers, capabilities & the future

Why giant data centers?

Companies and governments are building huge AI data centers, such as OpenAI's Stargate project and xAI's Colossus in Memphis. They serve two jobs: training new models, which takes the most compute, and running inference for millions of users. Power has become the bottleneck, so some builders are adding or buying their own power supply.

Does anyone know everything these models can do?

No, not even the labs.

Abilities sometimes appear that nobody specifically trained for, which is called emergent capability. Labs run evaluations before release, and users keep discovering new uses afterward. Progress has been fast, but whether it keeps compounding is genuinely debated. Some expect acceleration as AI helps build AI; others expect limits from power, data, and cost.

Speculation · possibilities, not predictions
  • Agents running small-business back offices around the clock (scheduling, billing, follow-ups, bookkeeping).
  • Personal AI with long-term memory of your projects, home, and health, running locally for privacy.
  • AI doing real science: proposing experiments, designing materials and drugs, running robotic labs.
  • Robots in homes and job sites that learn tasks by watching once.
  • Simulations of whole systems (a town, a business) to test decisions before making them.

12

Skynet is real (sort of)

The Skynet joke is a pop-culture shortcut for AI going rogue. Real programs that analyze metadata at scale are a clearer warning: where your data goes matters, which is one reason running AI locally matters.

In The Terminator (1984), Skynet is a military AI that turns on humanity. The name is real, and it is older than the movie.

The satellites

Skynet is the name of the UK Ministry of Defence's military communications satellites. The first, Skynet 1A, launched on a US rocket in November 1969, fifteen years before the film. The system is still running. The next-generation Skynet 6A, built by Airbus, is scheduled to launch in 2027. It is a communications network, not an AI.

The algorithm

In May 2015, The Intercept published NSA slides from the Snowden documents describing a program called SKYNET. It used machine learning on the phone metadata of about 55 million Pakistani mobile users. Metadata means who called whom, when, and from where, not what was said. SKYNET looked for people whose travel and calling patterns resembled those of known Al Qaeda couriers. The flagged behaviors included frequent trips between certain cities, swapping SIM cards or handsets, and receiving incoming calls only.

A June 2012 slide showed the "highest scoring" person as probably Ahmad Zaidan, Al Jazeera's Islamabad bureau chief. Zaidan is a journalist whose job meant interviewing Al Qaeda leaders, and he denied any membership. The document showed he was already on a US watch list, but it gave no evidence for that label.

To a pattern-matching system, a reporter who meets dangerous people can look exactly like a courier.

In 2016, data scientist Patrick Ball told Ars Technica that the NSA's testing was "ridiculously optimistic." The model was trained and tested on just seven known couriers. Even the best false-alarm rate in the slides, 0.008%, would still have wrongly flagged about 15,000 people out of Pakistan's population of about 192 million at the time, as Ars Technica calculated. Others, including a Guardian analysis, pointed out that the slides describe preliminary research. Public documents do not show how SKYNET's output was actually used.

The real lesson

The real risk isn't a chrome robot. It is quiet statistical systems making judgments about people from data they never knew was being collected. Even when content is encrypted, metadata can still reveal patterns of life. When these systems are wrong, they are wrong about real people, at scale. Understanding how they work is how you notice when they are making mistakes, and how you push back. That is also why running models on your own machine, and controlling what leaves it, is more than a convenience.

Key terms
Metadata
Data about communications (who, when, where), not their content.
Machine learning
Software that learns patterns from examples instead of following hand-written rules.
False positive
An innocent case wrongly flagged as a match.
Pattern of life
A profile of someone's routine built from their location and contact data.

Quick review

Answer out loud first, then tap a question to check.

Does chatting with a model retrain it?
No. Chatting is inference; the weights don't change. A cloud host may still keep or train on logs. Memory and instructions are fed back in as context.
Open-weight vs. fully open?
Open-weight shares the weights; fully open also shares training data and code (e.g., OLMo).
Name two permissive licenses, and one custom one.
Apache 2.0 and MIT; Meta's Llama license.
Who makes Qwen, Gemma, and Phi?
Alibaba, Google, Microsoft.
Three ways to customize a model without training one from scratch?
Community fine-tunes, RAG, your own fine-tuning/LoRA (plus context engineering).
Why prefer safetensors over pickle?
Safetensors can't run code when loaded; a malicious pickle can.
Why can a newer 14B model beat an older 70B model?
Better training methods and data. Age and specialty matter, not just size.
Why be skeptical of benchmark scores?
Test leakage, tuning for the test, and cherry-picking. Test on your own tasks.
What is the main hardware limit for local models?
GPU VRAM. The model must fit to run fast.
About how much memory does an 8B model need at Q4?
Roughly 5–6 GB, plus room for context.
Ollama vs. Open WebUI?
Ollama runs the model; Open WebUI is the chat interface.
Who is text-generation-webui ("oobabooga") for?
Tinkerers who want every setting, many formats, extensions, and LoRA training.
Can encryption hide that you're sending traffic?
No. It hides the content, not the fact, timing, or destination.
Describe split roles in one sentence.
The private model and data box stays offline, a separate box faces the internet, and a narrow bridge sits between them (segmentation and defense in depth).
Container vs. VM?
A container shares the host OS (light); a VM is a full simulated computer (stronger isolation).
Name the five context-engineering levers.
Instructions, memory, skills, tools/connectors, examples.
What are the five parts of a good agent skill?
Trigger, inputs, steps, output format, stop rules.
What does MCP standardize?
How AI apps connect to tools and data sources.
Why are AI data centers limited by power?
Training and serving models take enormous electricity; supply hasn't kept pace.
What is emergent capability?
An ability that appears without being specifically trained for.
Which came first: the UK's Skynet satellites or The Terminator?
The satellites. Skynet 1A launched in 1969; the film came out in 1984.
What did the NSA's SKYNET analyze, and why did critics worry?
Phone metadata (travel and calling patterns) of about 55 million Pakistani users, to flag suspected couriers. Critics warned that even tiny false-positive rates would wrongly flag thousands of people, and it flagged a journalist as its top match.
