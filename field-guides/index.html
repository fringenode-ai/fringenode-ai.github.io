The field guide series

This page lists all ten FringeNode field guides, in series order.

They are short, written guides to artificial intelligence (AI) engineering. Four guides are out now and link to their pages. The others are planned. Each planned guide shows a one-line promise and is marked Coming later.

Unit 1

Get oriented

Start with what a model is, then learn the words the later guides use.

Out now

Field Guide 01 · Understanding AI

Learn what a model is, how open-weight and closed models differ, how to run one locally, and why privacy, agents, and context engineering matter, before you try to build.

Read it

Out now

Field Guide 02 · The AI Jargon Field Guide

Learn the 19 terms to learn first for AI engineering and agent orchestration, in order, ending on context engineering, plus 78 more in one plain line each.

Read it

Unit 2

Pack the window

Learn to choose what a model sees for one job, and what to leave out.

Out now

Field Guide 03 · Context Engineering in Practice

Pack a focused window for one real job, step by step, and see why less often wins.

Read it

Unit 3

A small system you can trust

Build a small agent with safety designed in, then learn how to test a change.

Out now

Field Guide 04 · Your First Agent, Safely

Design the safety in first, then build a small agent that can read and draft but cannot send, delete, or spend.

Read it

Coming later

Field Guide 05 · Evals for Beginners

Write a small, honest test set for one job, and reuse it when you change the prompt, the context, or the model, so you can tell whether you got better or worse.

Unit 4

Your own knowledge

Give the model your own notes, and decide what is remembered.

Coming later

Field Guide 06 · Retrieval You Can Trust

Give the model your own notes for one job, with citations, and keep it from treating retrieved text as instructions.

Coming later

Field Guide 07 · Memory That Helps

Decide what is remembered across steps or sessions, what is forgotten, and what must never be stored.

Unit 5

Run it your way

Run a model on a machine you control, and know when a hosted model is a better fit.

Coming later

Field Guide 08 · Local Models in Practice

Run a small open-weight model on a machine you control, know what will actually fit, and be honest about when a hosted model is the better tool.

Unit 6

Wire and watch

Connect more than one step, then trace what the system did and what it cost.

Coming later

Field Guide 09 · Orchestration in Practice

Wire more than one step or helper together for one job, with clear handoffs and a human approval step, without a setup that is hard to follow.

Coming later

Field Guide 10 · Watch the Work

Trace a job from question to answer, see what it cost, catch a failure, and log enough to debug — without writing private data into a place it does not belong.
